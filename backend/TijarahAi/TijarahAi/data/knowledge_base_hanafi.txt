[SOURCE SCOPE]
This file is a concise retrieval layer drawn from the five supplied AAOIFI
standards. Clause references are included so answers can be traced to the
source PDFs. "Permissible" and "prohibited" below report the AAOIFI standard;
the filename does not make these independent Hanafi fatwas. Escalate a novel,
jurisdiction-specific, or person-specific case to a qualified Hanafi scholar.

[STANDARD]: AAOIFI Shari'ah Standard No. 09 - Ijarah and Ijarah Muntahia
Bittamleek
[CORE TOPIC]: Leasing the usufruct of property: residential and commercial
property, land, vehicles, equipment, machinery, computers, and subleases;
including lease-to-own (Ijarah Muntahia Bittamleek, "IMB").
[SCOPE BOUNDARY]: This standard covers operating leases of property and IMB,
whether the institution is lessor or lessee. It does NOT cover Sukuk al-Ijarah
or employment/labour contracts. Do not use this standard alone to answer
freelancing, employee, or independent-contractor questions.

[PERMISSIBLE CONDITIONS]:
- Ijarah transfers a specified halal usufruct for a specified period in return
  for specified halal consideration. The asset must be usable while its corpus
  is preserved.
- The lessor must own the identified asset, or the sub-lessor its usufruct,
  before executing the lease. A customer may promise to lease while the lessor
  acquires the asset; an actual Ijarah begins only after acquisition and actual
  or constructive possession.
- A direct lease or a master agreement with separate offer/acceptance for each
  transaction is valid. A seriousness deposit may secure a promise; only actual
  loss caused by breach may be retained. It may become advance rent after the
  lease is executed.
- State the lease term and commencement. Rent is due when the lessee can use
  the asset or the usufruct is made available, not merely on signature. It may
  be paid in advance or instalments and may be cash, goods, or a service.
- Fixed rent or a floating rent is allowed. For floating rent, specify the first
  period's rent and a clear, non-disputable benchmark formula with both a floor
  and ceiling. Past-due rent is debt and cannot later be increased.
- The use must be halal and suitable/customary. Examples expressly contemplated
  by the standard are a house for residence, a car for transport, and a computer
  for data storage. A lease with a non-Muslim is valid when the intended use is
  halal and the lessor has no reason to expect prohibited use.
- The lessee performs ordinary/operating maintenance and is liable for damage
  caused by misconduct or negligence. The lessor bears ownership risk, defects
  impairing use, major maintenance, and permissible insurance/takaful cost; the
  lessee may carry these out as the lessor's agent at the lessor's cost.
- Subleasing is allowed at the same, lower, or higher rent unless the owner has
  validly prohibited it or required approval. Time-sharing and leasing an
  undivided co-owned share are also recognised forms.
- Security (pledge, guarantee, assignment of rights) may secure due rent or
  lessee misconduct. A solvent late payer may promise a charity donation; it
  must go to charitable causes under Shari'ah supervision, not to the lessor.
- The parties may amend future rent by mutual agreement, terminate by mutual
  consent, renew, or provide a valid termination option. On destruction of an
  identified asset, future rent ends; on partial impairment the lessee may
  terminate or agree a rent adjustment.
- In IMB, the eventual title transfer must be evidenced in a document separate
  from the lease: an independent promise to sell (token, agreed, remaining-rent,
  or market-value consideration) or an independent promise to gift, including a
  gift contingent on final instalments.

[PROHIBITED / INVALID STRUCTURES]:
- Leasing an asset for known prohibited use: e.g., premises for riba-based
  business or prohibited goods, or a vehicle to carry prohibited merchandise.
- Executing an identified-asset lease before the lessor owns/possesses the
  asset; leasing the same asset concurrently to two lessees for the same period.
- Making the lessee bear the lessor's major-maintenance, ownership-risk, or
  insurance liability; charging the lessee an unanticipated insurance cost after
  rent is fixed.
- Increasing rent because of late payment. Foreclosed security cannot be used
  for rent not yet due or periods in which the lessee has not had the benefit.
- Requiring remaining instalments after total destruction of the identified
  leased asset without lessee fault.
- Combining the IMB lease and future sale/transfer into one document/contract,
  or making a present sale effective only on a future date.

[EVIDENCE]:
- Definition and scope: Standard 09, clauses 1 and Appendix C (Ijarah; IMB).
- Ownership, promise deposit, and acquisition: clauses 2/1-2/4 and 3/1-3/7.
- Contract, term, successive lease, time-share, and sublease: clauses 4/1-4/2.
- Halal use, homes/cars/computers, maintenance, risk, insurance, and rent:
  clauses 5/1/1-5/1/8 and 5/2/1-5/2/5.
- Security, late payment, charity undertaking: clauses 6/1-6/5.
- Destruction, termination, renewal: clauses 7/1-7/2.
- Separate IMB title-transfer undertaking: clauses 8/1-8/8.

[STANDARD]: AAOIFI Shari'ah Standard No. 10 - Salam and Parallel Salam
[CORE TOPIC]: Advance-paid forward sale of standardised goods: agricultural
procurement, inventory pre-orders, wholesale supply, and standard product
pre-orders. Parallel Salam is a separate back-to-back supply/sale arrangement.
[SCOPE BOUNDARY]: Salam is not the standard for a made-to-order manufacturing
or construction contract (Istisna' has a separate standard).

[PERMISSIBLE CONDITIONS]:
- Salam is a sale of a prescribed commodity for deferred delivery against an
  immediately paid price. Parties may use successive contracts under a master
  framework, but each Salam is concluded separately.
- Clearly specify the capital: currency, amount, and payment method; or, if
  capital is goods/usufruct, its kind, specifications, and quantity. Capital is
  normally paid in full at the contract sitting; a short delay of at most two or
  three days is exceptionally allowed if it is shorter than the delivery period.
- The future goods (al-muslam fihi) must be fungible/standardisable goods for
  which conformity can be specified and the seller held responsible. Specify
  quality, type, quantity (weight/measure/volume/count), delivery date, and,
  where needed, delivery place. Use custom and expert practice to remove material
  uncertainty.
- The goods must normally be available at the stated delivery place/date. Branded
  mass-produced standard units can qualify when regularly available.
- Mortgage, guarantee, or other permissible security may secure delivery.
- On due delivery, the buyer accepts conforming goods; superior non-vital quality
  is accepted without a price increase. Inferior goods may be rejected, accepted,
  or settled at a discount. Early conforming delivery is allowed unless the buyer
  has a valid reason to reject it.
- If goods are unavailable at maturity, the buyer may wait, cancel and recover
  paid capital, or agree after maturity to a valid substitution. Mutual full or
  partial cancellation for return of the corresponding capital is allowed.
- A seller or buyer may make a Parallel Salam with a third party for matching
  goods. Both contracts, parties' rights, and obligations must remain fully
  independent; default under one does not suspend or terminate the other.

[PROHIBITED / INVALID STRUCTURES]:
- Using an existing debt/loan owed by the seller as Salam capital.
- Selling the promised goods before taking possession.
- Contracting for a specified individual item ("this car"), land/buildings/trees,
  antiques/jewellery subject to individual assessment, or output from a named
  field/factory; requiring currency, gold, or silver as goods when capital is
  currency, gold, or silver.
- Leaving the goods, quantity, delivery date, or delivery location materially
  uncertain; a product pre-order without exact standardisable specifications.
- A penalty clause for the seller's late delivery.
- Linking the two contracts in Parallel Salam or making performance of one
  contingent on the other; issuing tradable Sukuk based on Salam debt.

[EVIDENCE]:
- Scope and contract framework: Standard 10, clauses 1 and 2/1-2/2.
- Capital and immediate payment: clause 3/1/1-3/1/4.
- Eligible goods, specifications, availability, time/place, security: clauses
  3/2/1-3/3.
- Possession, substitution, cancellation, delivery remedies: clauses 4/1-5/8.
- Independent Parallel Salam and non-tradable Salam-debt Sukuk: clauses 6/1-6/4
  and 7.
- Shari'ah basis in the supplied PDF: Qur'an 2:282; Ibn 'Abbas's report; and the
  hadith, "Whoever pays ... for deferred delivery" must specify scale, weight,
  and delivery date (Appendix B).

[STANDARD]: AAOIFI Shari'ah Standard No. 12 - Sharikah (Musharakah) and
Modern Corporations
[CORE TOPIC]: Co-founder and investor partnerships, professional/service firms,
joint ventures, companies, share capital, and diminishing co-ownership.

[PERMISSIBLE CONDITIONS]:
- Sharikat al-'Aqd is an agreement of two or more parties to combine assets,
  labour, or liabilities for profit. Covered forms include 'Inan (capital
  partnership), Wujuh (credit/reputation), A'mal (professional/service), stock
  companies, joint-liability companies, commendum, companies limited by shares,
  Muhassah, and diminishing Musharakah.
- Document offer/acceptance and the business objectives. Partnership with a
  non-Muslim or conventional bank is allowed for halal operations only when
  Shari'ah compliance is assured through management/supervision.
- Capital should normally be monetary and measurable. Tangible assets may be
  contributed after currency valuation; multiple currencies are translated at
  current exchange rates. Pure receivables alone cannot be capital.
- Profit must be an agreed undivided percentage of actual profit, set when the
  contract is concluded. It may differ from capital ratios, but the extra share
  cannot be stipulated for a sleeping partner. Loss is strictly pro rata to each
  partner's capital contribution.
- Partners hold partnership assets as trustees: liability is only for misconduct,
  negligence, or breach. Security for those failures is allowed. A truly
  independent third party may make a free, unlinked loss-of-capital guarantee
  subject to the standard's ownership-independence conditions.
- A managing partner cannot receive a fixed management fee inside the Sharikah
  contract, but may receive a larger profit share. A separate, independently
  terminable management contract may pay a partner a fixed fee. An outside
  manager may be paid fixed remuneration.
- In a service partnership (A'mal), the subject is services, not monetary
  capital. Partners may divide services unequally and share resulting profit by
  agreed ratio, but not allocate a lump sum out of profit to one partner.
- A stock company has separate legal personality and limited shareholder
  liability when this is publicly disclosed. New shares may be issued at fair
  value; subscriptions may be paid in instalments; a no-fee underwriting promise
  is allowed (fee only for separate actual services).
- Diminishing Musharakah: each partner contributes cash or valued tangible
  assets. The prospective buyer gives only an independent promise to buy units;
  every purchase/sale is independent of the partnership. Profit ratios are clear;
  losses follow changing equity ratios. Either partner may rent the other's share
  for stated rent and term.

[PROHIBITED / INVALID STRUCTURES]:
- Fixed profit, a percentage of capital as profit, or postponing the profit ratio
  until profit appears. Final distribution must use actual/constructive valuation,
  not expected profit.
- A partner guaranteeing another partner's capital or a diminishing-Musharakah
  buyout at original/face value. The buyout must use market value or price agreed
  at the time of acquisition.
- Using interest-bearing finance to buy shares, short-selling shares not owned,
  or preference/Tamattu' shares with prohibited financial priority.
- A sleeping partner managing a commendum/company limited by shares, or receiving
  a fixed amount/a percentage of capital as profit.
- Making the gradual buy/sell undertaking a condition of the diminishing
  partnership, forcing one partner to bear all insurance/maintenance because it
  will eventually own the asset, or giving either party a contractual right to
  withdraw its capital share.

[EVIDENCE]:
- Scope, definition, and all covered partnership forms: Standard 12, clauses
  1, 2/1-2/2.
- 'Inan formation, capital, management, guarantees, profit/loss, and exit:
  clauses 3/1/1-3/1/6.
- Wujuh and professional/service partnership: clauses 3/2-3/3.
- Modern company forms and share rules: clauses 4/1-4/5.
- Diminishing Musharakah: clauses 5/1-5/9.

[STANDARD]: AAOIFI Shari'ah Standard No. 21 - Financial Paper (Shares and
Bonds)
[CORE TOPIC]: Issuing, screening, buying, selling, pledging, and purifying
corporate shares; bonds, short selling, derivatives, and share-based contracts.
[SCOPE BOUNDARY]: This standard covers shares and interest-bearing bonds, not
investment Sukuk (which have a separate standard).

[PERMISSIBLE CONDITIONS]:
- A company and its shares are permissible when its stated objectives and core
  business are halal. A share represents an undivided share in the company and
  its assets/rights; spot or permitted-deferred trading is allowed for a halal
  company's shares after purchase formalities and transfer of liability.
- A mixed-activity company (core activity halal but incidental riba/prohibited
  dealings) is an AAOIFI exception only if all conditions are met: its charter
  does not make riba/prohibited goods an objective; interest-bearing debt is no
  more than 30% of market capitalisation; interest-bearing deposits are no more
  than 30% of market capitalisation; and prohibited income is no more than 5% of
  total income. Use the last budget/verified financial position.
- Purify the prohibited income attributable to owned shares at the financial
  period end. Formula: (company total prohibited income / total shares) x number
  of shares owned. Purification applies whether profit is distributed or not and
  whether the company reports profit or loss; do not use the prohibited component,
  including to pay taxes.
- Fixed, reasonable issuance expenses, fair-value new issues, instalment
  subscriptions, and no-fee underwriting are allowed. Fees may be paid for
  separate work (e.g., feasibility study/marketing), not the guarantee itself.
- Ordinary shares may carry procedural/administrative rights such as voting;
  lawful shares may be pledged. Licensed brokers may be required by regulators.
- Share trading must respect the composition of company assets: cash-only shares
  may trade only at nominal value with possession; debt-only shares follow debt
  rules; for a business mainly in tangible assets/benefits/rights, such assets,
  benefits, and rights must be at least 30% of total asset value for ordinary
  trading treatment.
- Investment Sukuk are the Shari'ah substitute identified for interest-bearing
  bonds.

[PROHIBITED / INVALID STRUCTURES]:
- Shares in a company whose objectives include riba, liquor, swine, or another
  prohibited activity; mixed-company investment that fails any listed condition.
- Preference shares with financial priority in liquidation/profit and Tamattu'
  shares as defined by the standard.
- Margin/interest loans to purchase shares; short selling; lending shares;
  Salam, futures, options, swaps, or rental of shares for market practices.
- Trading cash-only shares away from nominal value/possession, or debt-only
  shares without applying debt rules; using a small tangible component as a device
  to trade debts.
- Issuing, buying, selling, pledging, or endorsing interest-bearing bonds,
  including zero-coupon and prize bonds.

[EVIDENCE]:
- Scope and share issuance: Standard 21, clauses 1 and 2/1-2/8.
- Share nature, halal-company rule, mixed-company screening, and purification:
  clauses 3/1-3/4/6. The 30% debt/deposit caps and 5% income cap are clauses
  3/4/2-3/4/4; the per-share purification method is clause 3/4/6/4.
- Deal restrictions and asset-composition rules: clauses 3/6-3/20.
- Bonds and the Sukuk substitute: clauses 4-6.

[STANDARD]: AAOIFI Shari'ah Standard No. 23 - Agency and the Act of an
Uncommissioned Agent (Fodooli)
[CORE TOPIC]: Wakalah for buying, selling, receiving, paying, delivery,
brokerage, procurement, real-estate/fund/investment agency, affiliate-style
commissions, and agency-structured dropshipping.

[APPLICATION NOTE]: "Dropshipping" is a modern application label, not a term in
the standard. A compliant model should be an actual, documented agency for the
supplier or buyer: the agent acts within authorised scope, accounts to the
principal, and earns known remuneration. Do not label an agent as owner or sell
as principal without a valid separate ownership/sale structure.

[PERMISSIBLE CONDITIONS]:
- Agency is delegation by a principal to an agent for a delegable matter. It can
  be immediate, conditional, future, specific/general, limited/absolute,
  paid/unpaid, binding/non-binding, or temporary/continuous. Offer and acceptance
  can be verbal, written, by message, or customary gesture.
- The principal has legal capacity and the right to dispose of the subject. The
  agent has capacity and knows their agency status. The delegated matter is known
  enough to avoid material dispute, owned/controlled by the principal, legally
  delegable, and halal.
- In general/absolute agency, the agent observes the principal's interest and
  custom. Without prior consent, an absolute agent cannot sell below/buy above
  market price or make barter/deferred sales.
- Paid agency is valid. State remuneration as a lump sum, a share of a specified
  amount/output, or a formula linked to a future-known indicator; if omitted,
  the market rate for comparable work applies. A bonus may be added. An undefined
  right to take an unspecified share of the principal's entitlement is invalid.
- A paid agency is binding. Other binding cases include third-party rights,
  work that cannot stop without harm, and an undertaking not to revoke for a
  stated period.
- The principal bears procurement price and normal expenses (transport, storage,
  taxes, maintenance, insurance) and pays agreed remuneration. The agent is a
  trustee, liable only for misconduct, negligence, or breach.
- An agent may buy for the principal and then buy from the principal only through
  separate offer/acceptance that keeps agency and sale liabilities distinct.
  A sub-agent needs principal permission; multiple agents act jointly unless
  separately appointed or authorised otherwise.
- An unauthorised Fodooli transaction is subject to the owner's approval or
  rejection. Approval, before either party revokes, makes it effective
  retroactively from the act date.

[PROHIBITED / INVALID STRUCTURES]:
- Agency for a haram transaction (including prohibited commodities or riba).
- An agent dealing with themself, their dependent child, spouse, or partner in
  the same contract; acting for both sides; or appointing a sub-agent without
  permission, except where the standard expressly permits/authorises it.
- Combining the agent role and a personal guarantee in the same contract. In an
  investment agency this guarantees capital/proceeds and changes the arrangement
  into an interest-based loan. A voluntary, independent guarantee in another
  capacity is treated separately.
- Ignoring price, timing, possession, ownership, or other authorised limits. The
  principal may affirm or invalidate a non-beneficial breach; an agent who buys
  above market/authorised price or sells below it compensates the difference.

[EVIDENCE]:
- Scope, definition, formation, forms: Standard 23, clauses 1, 2/1-2/2.
- Party and subject conditions; types of agency: clauses 3/1-3/3 and 4/1.
- Paid/binding/temporary agency and remuneration: clauses 4/2-4/4.
- Principal/agent commitments and trustee liability: clauses 5/1-5/2.
- Self-dealing, breach, sub-agency, and multiple agents: clauses 6/1-6/5.
- Expiry and Fodooli: clauses 7/1-7/2 and 8/1-8/2.
- Shari'ah basis for known agency wage: hadith, "He who hires a hired worker
  must make known to him his wages" (Appendix B).
