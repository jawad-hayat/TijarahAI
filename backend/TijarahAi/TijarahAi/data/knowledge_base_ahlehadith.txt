[STANDARD]: General Principles of Business Transactions
[CORE TOPIC]: The legal basis, encouragement, and ethics of trade and earning
a livelihood in Islam according to the Ahle-Hadith school.

[PERMISSIBLE CONDITIONS]:
- Trade and buying/selling are permissible by the Qur'an, Sunnah and consensus
  of the Muslim Nation. Allah says: "Allah has permitted trading and forbidden
  interest" (Qur'an 2:275).
- The basic ruling concerning business transactions is that they are all
  permissible, as long as they are done with the approval of both parties and
  they have not been explicitly prohibited by the Lawgiver (Qur'an 4:29).
- Earning one's living through one's own hands is highly encouraged. The Prophet
  (peace be upon him) said: "No one consumes any food better than that which was
  earned by his own hands. The Prophet of Allah Dawood used to eat what his own
  hands earned." (Bukhari)
- The Prophet (peace be upon him) said: "It is better for one of you to bring a
  load of firewood on his back and sell it, than to ask of another who might give
  him or refuse to give him." (Bukhari, Tirmidhi, Nasa'i — Saheeh per al-Albani,
  Saheeh al-Jami' no. 7069)
- There is no harm in wealth as long as one maintains piety (taqwa). The Prophet
  (peace be upon him) said: "There is no harm in riches for the one who has piety.
  And health is better for the one who has piety." (Ibn Majah — Saheeh per
  al-Albani, Saheeh Sunan Ibn Majah no. 1741)
- Moderation in seeking livelihood is encouraged. The Prophet (peace be upon him)
  said: "O people, fear Allah and act properly in seeking wealth, for a soul
  will not die until it exhausts its sustenance, even if it is slow in coming.
  Fear Allah and act properly in seeking wealth by taking what is permissible
  and leaving what is forbidden." (Saheeh per al-Albani)
- Being easy-going in transactions is praised. The Prophet (peace be upon him)
  said: "May Allah have mercy on the one who is easy-going and generous while
  buying, while selling and when demanding his rights." (Bukhari — Saheeh per
  al-Albani, Saheeh al-Jami' no. 4454)
- Giving respite to one in straitened circumstances is virtuous. The Prophet
  (peace be upon him) said about a trader who used to excuse debtors: "Therefore,
  Allah overlooked his sins and forgave him." (Bukhari — Saheeh per al-Albani,
  Saheeh al-Jami' no. 3495)
- Seeking blessings in early hours for trade: "O Allah, bless my Nation in its
  early hours." (Ibn Majah, Tirmidhi, Abu Dawood — Saheeh per al-Albani)

[PROHIBITED / INVALID STRUCTURES]:
- Honesty is obligatory; deception is forbidden. The Prophet (peace be upon him)
  found a seller concealing a defect in grains and said: "Whoever deceives is
  not from us." (Ibn Majah, Abu Dawood, Tirmidhi, Muslim — Saheeh per al-Albani,
  Irwa' al-Ghaleel no. 1319)
- The two parties of a sale have the option of cancellation as long as they have
  not separated. "If they are honest with each other they will be blessed in
  their transaction. If they concealed or lied, then the blessings in their
  transactions will have been effaced." (Bukhari, Muslim, Abu Dawood, Tirmidhi,
  Nasa'i)
- A Muslim must not sell something defective to his brother without pointing
  that out to him. (Ibn Majah — Saheeh per al-Albani, Saheeh al-Jami' no. 6705)

[EVIDENCE]:
- Qur'an: 2:275, 4:29.
- Hadith of al-Miqdaam on earning (Bukhari, Saheeh al-Jami' no. 5546).
- Hadith of Abu Hurayrah on selling firewood (Bukhari, Saheeh al-Jami' no. 7069).
- Hadith on wealth and piety (Ibn Majah, Saheeh Sunan Ibn Majah no. 1741).
- Hadith of Jabir on moderation (Saheeh per al-Albani).
- Hadith of Hakeem ibn Hizam on honesty (Bukhari, Muslim).

[STANDARD]: Forbidden Business Transactions
[CORE TOPIC]: Transactions explicitly prohibited by the Lawgiver: overly risky
transactions (gharar), selling what one does not possess, selling before taking
possession, undercutting, al-'eenah, and payments over time with price increase.

[PERMISSIBLE CONDITIONS]:
- The prohibition of overly-risky transactions (gharar) is a great principle of
  the principles of business transactions. Imam an-Nawawi explained that
  countless issues are covered by it: sale of a runaway slave, of something that
  does not exist, of something unknown, of something one cannot deliver, of
  something not completely owned by the seller, of fish in a large body of water,
  of milk in an animal's udder, of a fetus in an animal, of an unspecified part
  of a heap, etc. All such sales are void.
- If due to some need, people must venture into risk-bearing transactions which
  cannot be avoided except with difficulty, and the amount of risk is miniscule,
  then the transaction is permissible. For example, buying a stuffed dish even
  if one does not see what it is stuffed with is permissible by consensus, while
  buying the stuffing by itself would not be permissible.

[PROHIBITED / INVALID STRUCTURES]:
- 1. Overly risky transactions (gharar): The Prophet (peace be upon him)
  prohibited bai' al-hasah (pebble sale) and overly-risky transactions.
  (Muslim, Tirmidhi, Abu Dawood, Ibn Majah, Nasa'i — Saheeh per al-Albani,
  Mukhtasar Saheeh Muslim no. 939, Irwa' al-Ghaleel no. 1294)
- Al-mulamasah transaction (touching a garment without inspecting it) and
  al-munabadhah transaction (flinging a garment to each other without
  inspection) are both prohibited. (Muslim — Saheeh per al-Albani, Mukhtasar
  Saheeh Muslim no. 938)
- Stud fees are prohibited. Ibn 'Umar said the Prophet (peace be upon him)
  forbade stud fees. (Bukhari, Abu Dawood, Tirmidhi — Saheeh per al-Albani,
  Mukhtasar Saheeh Muslim no. 1292)
- Selling the unborn fetus (habal al-hablah) is prohibited. (Bukhari, Muslim,
  Abu Dawood, Tirmidhi, Nasa'i, Ibn Majah)
- 2. Selling what one does not possess: Hakeem ibn Hazim asked the Prophet about
  selling something he does not have. The Prophet replied: "Do not sell that
  which you do not have." (Ibn Majah, Tirmidhi, Abu Dawood — Saheeh per
  al-Albani, Irwa' al-Ghaleel no. 1292)
- 3. Selling something before taking possession: Ibn 'Abbas narrated that the
  Messenger of Allah said: "Whoever buys foodstuff must not sell them until he
  takes possession of them." Ibn 'Abbas added: "I reckon that everything is
  analogous to foodstuff." (Bukhari, Muslim, Abu Dawood, Nasa'i, Tirmidhi)
- 4. Selling against one's brother (undercutting): "Do not undercut one another
  in business transactions." (Bukhari, Muslim, Ibn Majah) "A Muslim should not
  make a purchase in opposition to his brother." (Muslim — Saheeh per al-Albani,
  Irwa' al-Ghaleel no. 1298)
- 5. Al-'Eenah transaction (buying on credit then selling back to the original
  seller for a lower cash price — a riba circumvention device). Ibn 'Umar
  narrated the Prophet warned: "If you begin to participate in al-'eenah... Allah
  will inflict upon you a disgrace." (Abu Dawood — Saheeh per al-Albani, Saheeh
  al-Jami' no. 423)
- 6. Two transactions in one (hai' al-taqseet with riba): "Whoever has made a
  deal with two transactions in one shall receive the lesser of the two or riba
  (interest)." (Abu Dawood — Hasan per al-Albani, Saheeh al-Jami' no. 6116)

[EVIDENCE]:
- Hadith of Abu Hurayrah on gharar (Muslim, Mukhtasar no. 939).
- Hadith on mulamasah/munabadhah (Bukhari, Muslim).
- Hadith of Hakeem ibn Hazim on selling what one does not possess (Irwa' no. 1292).
- Hadith of Ibn 'Abbas on possession before resale (Bukhari, Muslim).
- Hadith on undercutting (Bukhari, Muslim).
- Hadith on al-'eenah (Abu Dawood, Saheeh al-Jami' no. 423).
- Hadith on two-in-one deals (Abu Dawood, Saheeh al-Jami' no. 6116).

[STANDARD]: Items Prohibited to Sell
[CORE TOPIC]: Specific items and categories whose sale is forbidden: alcohol,
carrion, swine, idols, dogs, images of living creatures, unripe produce.

[PROHIBITED / INVALID STRUCTURES]:
- 1. Alcohol: 'A'ishah said: "When the last verses of Soorah al-Baqarah were
  revealed, the Prophet went out and said, 'Trade in alcohol has been
  forbidden.'" (Bukhari, Muslim, Abu Dawood, Nasa'i)
- 2. Carrion, Swine and Idols: "Allah and His Messenger have forbidden the sale
  of alcohol, carrion, swine and idols." Including the fat of carrion — "No, it
  is forbidden." The Prophet also said: "May Allah destroy the Jews. Allah
  forbade them its fat but they collected it together, sold it and devoured its
  price." (Bukhari, Muslim, Tirmidhi, Abu Dawood, Ibn Majah, Nasa'i)
- 3. Dogs: Abu Mas'ood al-Ansari narrated that the Messenger of Allah prohibited
  the price of a dog, the wages of a prostitute and the earnings of a soothsayer.
  (Bukhari, Muslim, Abu Dawood, Tirmidhi, Ibn Majah, Nasa'i)
- 4. Pictures or Statues Containing Creatures with Souls: The Prophet said:
  "Whoever makes a picture will be punished by Allah until he blows a soul into
  it and he will never be able to do so." Ibn 'Abbas advised: "If you insist on
  doing such work, then make things like trees — everything that does not contain
  a soul." (Bukhari, Muslim, Nasa'i)
- 5. Dates/Fruits Before they Become Ripe: The Prophet forbade the sale of dates
  until their ripeness had become apparent. "Don't you see that if Allah keeps
  the fruits from ripening, how will you take the money from your brother?"
  (Bukhari, Muslim, Nasa'i — Saheeh per al-Albani, Saheeh al-Jami' no. 6928)
- 6. Crops Before Grains become Husked: The Prophet forbade the sale of date-palms
  until they ripened and ears of corn until they were white and free from diseases.
  He forbade that for both the buyer and the seller. (Muslim, Abu Dawood,
  Tirmidhi, Nasa'i — Saheeh per al-Albani, Mukhtasar no. 917)

[EVIDENCE]:
- Hadith of 'A'ishah on alcohol trade (Bukhari, Muslim).
- Hadith of Jabir on carrion/swine/idols (Bukhari, Muslim).
- Hadith on dog prices (Bukhari, Muslim).
- Hadith of Ibn 'Abbas on images (Bukhari, Muslim).
- Hadith of Anas on unripe dates (Bukhari, Saheeh al-Jami' no. 6928).
- Hadith of Ibn 'Umar on unripe crops (Muslim, Mukhtasar no. 917).

[STANDARD]: Options in Sale Contracts (Khiyar)
[CORE TOPIC]: The right of cancellation after a sale: session option, stipulated
option, and option due to defect.

[PERMISSIBLE CONDITIONS]:
- 1. Session option (khiyar al-majlis): Both contracting parties have an option
  to cancel the contract from the time the contract is concluded until they
  separate from one another. Ibn 'Umar narrated: "If two people make a sale,
  then each of them has the option as long as they have not parted." (Bukhari,
  Muslim, Nasa'i)
- 2. Stipulated option: Both parties or one party may lay down a stipulation
  that they will have the option for a specified period of time to cancel the
  agreement. This is valid even if the time period stated is long. (Bukhari,
  Muslim, Nasa'i)
- 3. Option due to defect: If a person purchases an item and does not realize it
  is defective until after departure, he has the right to return the item to the
  seller. Abu Hurayrah reported: "Whoever bought a goat that was left unmilked
  [to make it seem more productive] and then milks it has a choice: if pleased,
  keep it; if displeased, return it with a sa' of dates for the milk consumed."
  (Bukhari, Muslim, Abu Dawood, Nasa'i)

[PROHIBITED / INVALID STRUCTURES]:
- It is forbidden to hurriedly leave the session out of fear that the other party
  will cancel the transaction. "It is not permissible for one of them to separate
  from the other for fear that one may demand the bargain be rescinded." (Abu
  Dawood, Tirmidhi, Nasa'i — Saheeh per al-Albani, Saheeh al-Jami' no. 2895)
- Do not leave camels or goats unmilked to make it seem they produce more milk
  (an-najsh / at-tasriyah). (Bukhari, Saheeh al-Jami' no. 7347)

[EVIDENCE]:
- Hadith of Ibn 'Umar on session option (Bukhari, Muslim).
- Hadith of 'Amr ibn Shu'ayb on leaving the session (Saheeh al-Jami' no. 2895).
- Hadith of Abu Hurayrah on musarrah (Bukhari, Muslim).

[STANDARD]: Riba (Interest/Usury)
[CORE TOPIC]: The absolute prohibition of riba, its categories (riba
an-nasee'ah and riba al-fadl), the six ribawi commodities, and exchange rules.

[PERMISSIBLE CONDITIONS]:
- If items of the same genus are exchanged (gold for gold, dates for dates, etc.)
  they must be equivalent in weight or amount, regardless of any differences in
  quality, and the exchange must take place on the spot. (Muslim, Mukhtasar no.
  939)
- If one wants to exchange one commodity for another of a different type (e.g.
  gold for silver, or barley for wheat), this is permissible as long as the
  exchange takes place at once (hand to hand), and the quantities may differ.
  (Muslim, Irwa' al-Ghaleel vol. 5 p. 195)
- If one trades any of the six commodities for another which has a different
  purpose (e.g. gold for barley, or silver for salt), it is allowed for the
  quantities to differ as well as for the payment to be made over time. The
  Prophet bought foodstuff from a Jew on credit and gave him his shield as
  collateral. (Bukhari, Irwa' al-Ghaleel no. 1393)
- Al-'Araya exception: The poor who do not have date-palms are allowed to sell
  the fresh dates on the trees in exchange for an estimated amount of dried
  dates. (Muslim, Bukhari)
- It is not allowed to exchange a riba commodity with its own genus combined with
  something else (e.g. gold mixed with pearls for gold alone). "It should not be
  sold until its components have been separated." (Abu Dawood, Ibn Majah, Nasa'i,
  Tirmidhi — Saheeh per al-Albani, Irwa' al-Ghaleel no. 1356)

[PROHIBITED / INVALID STRUCTURES]:
- Riba is forbidden by the Qur'an, Sunnah and consensus of the Nation.
  (Qur'an 2:275-279, 2:276)
- The Prophet said: "Avoid the seven destructive sins" — and listed consuming
  interest among them. (Bukhari, Muslim, Abu Dawood, Nasa'i)
- The Prophet cursed the receiver of interest, the one who gives it, the one
  who records it and its two witnesses, saying: "They are all the same."
  (Muslim — Saheeh per al-Albani, Mukhtasar no. 955)
- "Riba has seventy-three varieties, the slightest of which is like a man having
  intercourse with his mother." (al-Hakim — Saheeh per al-Albani, Saheeh
  al-Jami' no. 3539)
- "One gold coin of riba that a person knowingly consumes is worse than
  thirty-six acts of fornication." (Ahmad — Saheeh per al-Albani, Saheeh
  al-Jami' no. 3375)
- Riba an-Nasee'ah: A stipulated increase in payments received by the creditor
  from the debtor due to delaying the payment. Forbidden by Qur'an, Sunnah and
  consensus.
- Riba al-Fadl: Exchanging money for money or food for food in a spot transaction
  but with unequal values. Forbidden by Sunnah and consensus.
- The six riba commodities: Gold, silver, wheat, barley, dates, and salt.
  "Gold exchanged for gold, silver for silver, wheat for wheat, barley for
  barley, dates for dates and salt for salt must be for the same amounts,
  equivalent and hand to hand." (Muslim — Saheeh per al-Albani)
- "Do not sell gold for gold unless equivalent in weight... do not sell a lesser
  amount for a greater amount or vice-versa... do not sell gold or silver not
  present at the moment of exchange for gold or silver that is present."
  (Bukhari, Muslim, Nasa'i)
- It is not permissible to sell fresh dates for dry dates due to weight loss
  upon drying. (Abu Dawood, Irwa' al-Ghaleel no. 1352)

[EVIDENCE]:
- Qur'an: 2:275-279, 2:276, 22:5.
- Hadith of Abu Hurayrah on seven destructive sins (Bukhari, Muslim).
- Hadith of Jabir on cursing riba participants (Muslim, Saheeh al-Jami' no. 5090).
- Hadith of 'Ubadah ibn as-Samit on six commodities (Muslim, Mukhtasar no. 939).
- Hadith of Abu Sa'eed on equivalent exchange (Bukhari, Muslim).
- Hadith of 'Umar on gold/silver exchange (Bukhari, Muslim).
- Hadith of Sa'd on fresh vs dried dates (Irwa' no. 1352).
- Hadith of Fadalah on mixed gold/pearls (Irwa' no. 1356).

[STANDARD]: Sharecropping (al-Muzara'ah) and Tree-Tending (al-Musaqah)
[CORE TOPIC]: Farming arrangements where land or trees are given to another to
cultivate/tend in exchange for a share of the produce.

[PERMISSIBLE CONDITIONS]:
- Sharecropping is permissible. The Prophet had the people of Khaybar work the
  fields with each getting half the produce. (Bukhari, Muslim, Abu Dawood, Ibn
  Majah, Tirmidhi)
- All Emigrants in Madeenah used to cultivate the land of the Ansar for a third
  or fourth of the yield. Major Companions including 'Ali, S'ad, Ibn Mas'ood,
  'Umar ibn 'Abdul-'Azeez, and the families of Abu Bakr, 'Umar, and 'Ali all
  did sharecropping.
- Either the land owner, the sharecropper, or both may provide the capital needs
  (seeds, tools, etc.). 'Umar would make agreements with different provisions
  for who supplied seeds. (Bukhari)
- Al-Musaqah (tree-tending): Giving another person trees to attend, water and
  care for in exchange for half or a specific portion of their yield. The Prophet
  had the people of Khaybar do this. (Bukhari, Muslim, Abu Dawood)

[PROHIBITED / INVALID STRUCTURES]:
- It is not allowed to sharecrop by saying that the owner will receive the yield
  of a specific portion of the land while the sharecropper receives the yield
  of another specific portion. (Bukhari, Irwa' al-Ghaleel vol. 5 p. 299)
- It is not allowed to say to the land owner: "I will get such and such produce
  regardless of the total produce."
- Renting land for uncertain portions of its produce is prohibited. However, it
  is permissible to rent land for gold, silver coins, or anything definite and
  reliable as payment. (Muslim, Abu Dawood, Nasa'i — Saheeh per al-Albani)

[EVIDENCE]:
- Hadith of Ibn 'Umar on Khaybar (Bukhari, Muslim).
- Statements of Bukhari on Companions' practice.
- Hadith of Rafi' ibn Khadeej on impermissible forms (Bukhari, Irwa' vol. 5).
- Hadith of Abu Hurayrah on Ansar and Emigrants tree-sharing (Bukhari, Muslim,
  Irwa' no. 1471).

[STANDARD]: Reviving Dead Lands
[CORE TOPIC]: Cultivating unclaimed, uncultivated land and the rights established
by doing so (similar to homesteading).

[PERMISSIBLE CONDITIONS]:
- "Whoever cultivates land that does not belong to anyone has the most right to
  it." 'Umar gave that ruling during his caliphate. (Bukhari — Saheeh per
  al-Albani, Saheeh al-Jami' no. 6058)
- "If someone revives a dead land, it will be for him." (Tirmidhi — Saheeh per
  al-Albani, Saheeh al-Jami' no. 5975)
- "If anyone surrounds a land with a wall, it belongs to him." (Abu Dawood —
  Saheeh per al-Albani, Saheeh al-Jami' no. 5952)

[EVIDENCE]:
- Hadith of 'A'ishah on cultivating unclaimed land (Bukhari, Saheeh al-Jami'
  no. 6058).
- Hadith of Jabir on reviving dead land (Tirmidhi, Saheeh al-Jami' no. 5975).
- Hadith on surrounding land with a wall (Abu Dawood, Saheeh al-Jami' no. 5952).

[STANDARD]: Leasing and Hiring (al-Ijarah)
[CORE TOPIC]: Renting property, hiring workers, the obligation to pay wages,
and impermissible forms of hiring.

[PERMISSIBLE CONDITIONS]:
- Al-Ijarah (leasing/hiring) is sanctioned by the Qur'an, Sunnah and consensus.
  Qur'an references: 65:6 (wages for wet-nurses), 28:26 (hiring the strong and
  trustworthy), 28:27 (serving for a period), 18:77 (taking wages for work).
- Anything that has a usufruct value while retaining its sound state is
  permissible to be hired or rented, as long as there is no specific prohibition
  in Islamic Law.
- The object to be hired must be specified, the payment must be specified, and
  the period of time/nature of work must be specified. (Qur'an 28:27)
- Renting land for gold, silver, or anything definite and reliable is
  permissible. (Muslim, Abu Dawood, Nasa'i — Saheeh per al-Albani, Irwa'
  al-Ghaleel vol. 5 p. 302)
- 'A'ishah narrated that the Prophet and Abu Bakr hired a guide from the tribe
  of ad-Dayl during their emigration. (Bukhari — Saheeh per al-Albani, Irwa'
  no. 1489)

[PROHIBITED / INVALID STRUCTURES]:
- Workers' wages must be paid promptly: "Give the wage-earner his wages before
  his sweat dries." (Ibn Majah — Saheeh per al-Albani, Saheeh Sunan Ibn Majah
  no. 198)
- The sin of not paying workers' wages: "Allah said: 'I will be a disputant
  against three persons on the Day of Resurrection: a man who makes a covenant
  with Me and then commits treachery; a man who sells a free person and consumes
  his price; and a man who hires a wage-earner and gets the full work from him
  but does not give him his wages.'" (Bukhari — Hasan per al-Albani, Irwa' no.
  1489)
- Forcing maids into prostitution for gain is forbidden (Qur'an 24:33).
- The price of a dog, the wages of a prostitute, and the earnings of a
  soothsayer are all prohibited. (Bukhari, Muslim)
- Stud fees are prohibited. (Bukhari, Abu Dawood, Tirmidhi)
- Living off the Qur'an for worldly gain: "Read the Qur'an and do not live off
  of it nor amass a lot by it." (Ahmad — Saheeh per al-Albani, Saheeh al-Jami'
  no. 1168)

[EVIDENCE]:
- Qur'an: 65:6, 28:26-27, 18:77, 24:33.
- Hadith of Ibn 'Umar on wages (Saheeh Sunan Ibn Majah no. 198).
- Hadith of Abu Hurayrah on Allah's disputants (Bukhari).
- Hadith of 'A'ishah on hiring a guide (Bukhari, Irwa' no. 1489).
- Hadith on impermissible wages (Bukhari, Muslim).

[STANDARD]: Partnerships (ash-Sharikah)
[CORE TOPIC]: The Islamic partnership, its legality, profit-sharing, and
general rules of partnership in the Ahle-Hadith tradition.

[PERMISSIBLE CONDITIONS]:
- A partnership is where two or more persons combine together to obtain profit.
  It can even occur without intent, such as when one inherits such a situation.
  (Fath al-Bari, vol. 5 p. 129)
- Partnership is established by the Qur'an: "Verily, many partners oppress one
  another, except those who believe and do righteous good deeds, and they are
  few." (Qur'an 38:24) Also: partners in inheritance (Qur'an 4:12).
- As-Sa'ib said to the Prophet: "You were my partner in the pre-Islamic Days
  and you were the best of partners. You would never oppose me or argue with
  me." (Ibn Majah — Saheeh per al-Albani, Saheeh Sunan Ibn Majah no. 1853)
- The Islamic partnership comes into existence by the approval of two or more
  who agree that each will contribute a specific amount of wealth seeking
  earnings and profit. Each will receive from the profits according to the
  percentage contributed. By the same proportion they will be responsible for the
  capital.
- It is permissible for partners to agree that each will receive the same
  percentage of profit even though the percentage of contributions differed,
  as trade is based on mutual approval. (Ash-Shawkani, as-Sail al-Jarrar)

[EVIDENCE]:
- Qur'an: 38:24, 4:12.
- Hadith of as-Sa'ib (Ibn Majah, Saheeh Sunan Ibn Majah no. 1853).
- Ash-Shawkani's ruling in as-Sail al-Jarrar vol. 3 pp. 246-248.

[STANDARD]: Silent Partnership (al-Mudarabah)
[CORE TOPIC]: A contract where one provides capital and another provides labour
for trade, with profits divided by mutual agreement. Also known as qarad.

[PERMISSIBLE CONDITIONS]:
- Al-Mudarabah is a contract between two parties where one gives money to the
  other to trade/do business with, and the profit is divided between them
  according to what they agree upon. (Qur'an 73:20)
- Ibn al-Mundhir wrote in al-Ijma' (p. 124): "The scholars all agree that
  qarad (silent partnership) by contributing only cash is permissible. They also
  all agree that the labourer can give any condition concerning the distribution
  of profits that they agree upon, whether it be one-third, one-half or
  whatever, as long as it is something known and a fixed portion."
- The Companions practiced this arrangement. The story of 'Abdullah and
  'Ubaydullah, sons of 'Umar, who received money from Abu Moosa to trade with
  and were made to share the profit with 'Umar as a silent partnership, is
  narrated by Malik and al-Bayhaqi. (Saheeh per al-Albani, Irwa' al-Ghaleel
  vol. 5 p. 291)
- The labourer is a trustee and is not responsible for the capital unless he
  transgresses customary behaviour or contravenes the agreement. (Ibn al-Mundhir,
  al-Ijma' p. 125)
- The owner of the capital may prohibit the labourer from selling on credit. If
  the labourer does sell on credit against this prohibition, he is liable for
  that money.
- Hakeem ibn Hizam used to stipulate upon his labourer: "Do not use my money
  to purchase any animals, do not carry it over the seas and do not stay with
  it in a flooded valley. If you do any of those, you are liable for my money."
  (ad-Daraqutni, al-Bayhaqi — Saheeh per al-Albani, Irwa' vol. 5 p. 293)

[EVIDENCE]:
- Qur'an: 73:20.
- Consensus recorded by Ibn al-Mundhir, al-Ijma' pp. 124-125.
- Narration of 'Umar's sons and Abu Moosa (Malik, al-Bayhaqi, Irwa' vol. 5
  p. 291).
- Stipulation of Hakeem ibn Hizam (Irwa' vol. 5 p. 293).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

[STANDARD]: Advance Payment / Forward Purchase (as-Salam / as-Salaf)
[CORE TOPIC]: Purchasing by advanced payment of described merchandise that the
seller must procure at a later date.

[PERMISSIBLE CONDITIONS]:
- As-Salam is sanctioned by the Qur'an: "O you who believe! When you contract
  a debt for a fixed period, write it down." (Qur'an 2:282)
- Ibn 'Abbas stated: "I bear witness that a guaranteed forward payment for a
  fixed period has been permitted by Allah in His Book." (al-Hakim, al-Bayhaqi —
  Saheeh per al-Albani, Irwa' al-Ghaleel no. 1369)
- When the Prophet came to Madeenah, people dealt in future purchases for dates
  for two and three years. The Prophet said: "Whoever makes a forward purchase
  of anything must do so for a specified measurement, specified weight and for
  a specified period of time." (Bukhari, Muslim, Tirmidhi, Abu Dawood, Ibn
  Majah, Nasa'i)
- It is not a condition that the seller actually owns the merchandise at the
  time of the advance payment. The Companions used to pay in advance to the
  peasants of Greater Syria for wheat, barley and olive oil for a specified
  measure to be delivered in a known period, without asking whether they had
  standing crops. (Bukhari, Abu Dawood, Nasa'i, Ibn Majah — Saheeh per
  al-Albani, Irwa' no. 1370)

[EVIDENCE]:
- Qur'an: 2:282.
- Hadith of Ibn 'Abbas on salam (Bukhari, Muslim).
- Hadith of 'Abdullah ibn Abu Awfa on advance payment practice (Bukhari, Irwa'
  no. 1370).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

[STANDARD]: Loans (al-Qard)
[CORE TOPIC]: The virtue of giving loans, warnings about debt, obligation to
repay, and the prohibition of interest-bearing loans.

[PERMISSIBLE CONDITIONS]:
- Giving loans is virtuous: "Whoever relieves a believer's distress of the
  distressful aspects of this world, Allah will rescue him from a difficulty of
  the Hereafter." (Muslim, Tirmidhi, Abu Dawood — Saheeh per al-Albani,
  Mukhtasar no. 1888)
- "No Muslim gives to another Muslim twice [i.e. lends twice] except that it
  will be like giving him charity once." (Ibn Majah — Hasan per al-Albani,
  Irwa' al-Ghaleel no. 1389)
- Repaying debt in the best manner is commendable: The Prophet received back
  a better camel than the one lent, saying: "The best among you is he who repays
  others in the best manner (generously)." (Bukhari, Muslim, Tirmidhi, Nasa'i)
- Seeking one's due should be done with decency: "Whoever demands a right
  should demand it with decency, whether it is fulfilled or not." (Ibn Majah —
  Saheeh per al-Albani, Saheeh Sunan Ibn Majah no. 1965)
- Giving respite to one in straitened circumstances: "And if the debtor is in
  a hard time, then grant him time till it is easy for him to repay." (Qur'an
  2:280) "Whoever would be pleased to have Allah save him from a distress on
  the Day of Resurrection should relieve one in straitened circumstances or
  completely remove his debt." (Ibn Majah — Saheeh per al-Albani)

[PROHIBITED / INVALID STRUCTURES]:
- Strong warnings about being in debt: "Whosoever's soul leaves his body while
  he is free of three things will enter Paradise: arrogance, treachery and debt."
  (Ibn Majah, Tirmidhi — Saheeh per al-Albani, Saheeh Sunan Ibn Majah no. 1956)
- "The soul of a believer is suspended due to his debt until someone fulfills it
  on his behalf." (Tirmidhi — Saheeh per al-Albani, Saheeh al-Jami' no. 6779)
- Even martyrdom does not cancel debt — Gabriel informed the Prophet of this.
  (Muslim, Tirmidhi, Nasa'i — Saheeh per al-Albani, Irwa' no. 1197)
- Taking people's wealth without intending to repay: "Whoever takes the wealth
  of the people intending to repay it, Allah will repay it on his behalf.
  Whoever takes it intending to destroy it, then Allah will destroy him."
  (Bukhari — Saheeh per al-Albani, Saheeh al-Jami' no. 598)
- "Anyone who takes a loan and is not intending to repay it will meet Allah as
  a thief." (Ibn Majah — Hasan Saheeh per al-Albani, Saheeh Sunan Ibn Majah
  no. 1954)
- Delaying repayment when one has the means is wrongdoing: "The delaying of
  repayment by one who has the means is a form of dhulm (wrongdoing)." (Bukhari,
  Muslim, Abu Dawood, Tirmidhi, Nasa'i, Ibn Majah)
- Every loan that entails a benefit contains interest. 'Abdullah ibn Salam
  warned: "One of the varieties of riba is for someone to make a loan for a
  period of time. When that time is reached, he gives him his money with a
  receptacle containing a gift. Beware of that receptacle and what it contains."
  (Bukhari, al-Bayhaqi — Saheeh per al-Albani, Irwa' vol. 5 p. 235)

[EVIDENCE]:
- Qur'an: 2:280, 4:58.
- Hadith of Abu Hurayrah on relieving distress (Muslim, Mukhtasar no. 1888).
- Hadith on debt and soul (Tirmidhi, Saheeh al-Jami' no. 6779).
- Hadith on repaying generously (Bukhari, Muslim).
- Hadith on loan-with-gift as riba (Bukhari, Irwa' vol. 5 p. 235).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

[STANDARD]: Pawning (ar-Rahn)
[CORE TOPIC]: Pledging collateral for a loan, its permissibility, and rules
regarding the use of the pawned item.

[PERMISSIBLE CONDITIONS]:
- Pawning is sanctioned by the Qur'an: "And if you are on a journey and cannot
  find a scribe, then let there be a pledge taken." (Qur'an 2:283) — but it
  is permissible even while not travelling.
- 'A'ishah narrated that the Prophet bought foodstuff from a Jew on credit and
  gave him his shield as collateral. (Bukhari — Saheeh per al-Albani, Irwa'
  al-Ghaleel no. 1393)
- If the pawned item is an animal that can be ridden or milked, the pawnbroker
  can ride the animal or milk it if he feeds it and maintains it. "The pledged
  animal can be ridden if it is pledged as long as the pawnbroker provides for
  it and the milk can be drunk as long as the pawnbroker provides for it."
  (Bukhari, Abu Dawood, Tirmidhi, Ibn Majah — Saheeh per al-Albani, Saheeh
  al-Jami' no. 3962)

[PROHIBITED / INVALID STRUCTURES]:
- It is not permissible for the pawnbroker to use what has been pawned with him
  (other than the animal exception), because any loan that entails benefit is
  a form of riba.
- A "Muslim pawnbroker" makes a loan and takes an item as collateral but does
  NOT charge interest (unlike conventional pawnbroking).

[EVIDENCE]:
- Qur'an: 2:283.
- Hadith of 'A'ishah on the Prophet's shield (Bukhari, Irwa' no. 1393).
- Hadith of Abu Hurayrah on riding/milking pawned animals (Bukhari, Saheeh
  al-Jami' no. 3962).

[STANDARD]: Debt Transfer (al-Hawalah)
[CORE TOPIC]: Transferring a debt from one person to another.

[PERMISSIBLE CONDITIONS]:
- If a person is in debt and can have another who has the means take over the
  debt, it is obligatory on the creditor to accept the transfer as long as the
  new debtor has the means.
- The Prophet said: "Delay in payment by one of means is a form of wrongdoing.
  If one of you has his debt transferred to a rich person, let him accept the
  transfer." (Ibn Majah — Saheeh per al-Albani, Saheeh al-Jami' no. 5876)

[EVIDENCE]:
- Hadith on debt transfer (Ibn Majah, Saheeh al-Jami' no. 5876).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

[STANDARD]: Depositing for Safekeeping (al-Wadee'ah)
[CORE TOPIC]: Depositing something with someone for safekeeping and the
liability rules.

[PERMISSIBLE CONDITIONS]:
- If a Muslim asks his brother to deposit something with him for safekeeping,
  it is recommended to accept if he has the means to safeguard it.
- It is obligatory to return the deposited item whenever the depositor requests
  it. (Qur'an 4:58)
- "Return the trust to the one who entrusted you." (Tirmidhi, Abu Dawood —
  Saheeh per al-Albani, Saheeh al-Jami' no. 240)
- The one entrusted with a deposit is NOT liable unless he was negligent. "If
  one accepts a deposit, there is no liability upon him." (Ibn Majah — Hasan
  per al-Albani, Saheeh Sunan Ibn Majah no. 1945, Irwa' no. 1547)
- "There is no liability upon the one who accepts a trust." (ad-Daraqutni,
  al-Bayhaqi — Hasan per al-Albani, Saheeh al-Jami' no. 7518)
- 'Umar ibn al-Khattab repaid a depositor for a deposit that had been stolen
  from among his wealth — understood as meaning he was negligent and therefore
  liable. (al-Bayhaqi, as-Sunan al-Kubra vol. 6 p. 289)

[EVIDENCE]:
- Qur'an: 4:58.
- Hadith on returning trusts (Saheeh al-Jami' no. 240).
- Hadith on no liability for trustee (Saheeh al-Jami' no. 7518).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

[STANDARD]: Lending Items at No Charge (al-'Ariyah)
[CORE TOPIC]: Allowing another to use an item without any payment or
compensation, and the obligation to return it.

[PERMISSIBLE CONDITIONS]:
- Lending is a commendable act. "Help you one another in piety and
  righteousness." (Qur'an 5:2)
- "Allah is helping the servant as long as the servant is helping his brother."
  (Muslim, Tirmidhi, Abu Dawood — Saheeh per al-Albani, Mukhtasar no. 1888)
- It is obligatory to return the borrowed item. "Verily! Allah commands that you
  should render back the trusts to those to whom they are due." (Qur'an 4:58)
- The borrower is a trustee; there is no liability unless he is negligent or if
  the owner lays down such a condition. The Prophet said to Ya'la: "Is it a loan
  with a guarantee on it or a loan simply to be paid back?" He replied: "A loan
  to be paid back." (Abu Dawood — Saheeh per al-Albani, Saheeh Sunan Abi Dawood
  no. 630, Silsilat as-Saheehah no. 3045)

[PROHIBITED / INVALID STRUCTURES]:
- Refusing small kindnesses (al-ma'un) is censured by Allah: "So woe unto those
  performers of prayers, who delay their prayer, those who do good deeds only to
  be seen, and refuse small kindnesses." (Qur'an 107:4-7)

[EVIDENCE]:
- Qur'an: 5:2, 4:58, 107:4-7.
- Hadith on helping one's brother (Muslim, Mukhtasar no. 1888).
- Hadith of Ya'la on guaranteed vs. simple loan (Abu Dawood, Silsilat no. 3045).

[STANDARD]: Lost and Found (al-Luqatah)
[CORE TOPIC]: Rules for finding lost wealth, animals, and items; obligations
of the finder.

[PERMISSIBLE CONDITIONS]:
- If someone finds lost wealth, he must note its type and amount, take a
  trustworthy person as a witness, safeguard the wealth, and announce it for
  one year. If the owner claims it by properly identifying its signs, he must be
  given the wealth. Otherwise, the finder may benefit from it. (Bukhari, Muslim,
  Tirmidhi, Ibn Majah, Abu Dawood)
- "Whoever finds a lost item should have one or two trustworthy people witness
  it. Then he should not change it or conceal it. If its owner comes, he has the
  most right to it." (Ibn Majah, Abu Dawood — Saheeh per al-Albani, Saheeh
  Sunan Ibn Majah no. 2032)
- Lost sheep/goat: The finder should take the animal and announce. If no owner
  comes, the finder becomes the owner: "It is going to be for you, your brother
  or the wolf." (Bukhari, Muslim)
- Lost camel: One should NOT take a lost camel. "What do you have to do with it?
  It has its feet, its supply of water and it can reach places of water and eat
  shrubbery." (Bukhari, Muslim)
- Edible or insignificant items found on the path may be taken without
  announcement. The Prophet said about some dates: "If I did not fear that they
  had been given in charity, I would have eaten them." (Bukhari, Muslim)
- Lost items in the precincts of the inviolable mosques (Makkah) can only be
  picked up if they are to be announced perpetually. (Bukhari — Saheeh per
  al-Albani, Saheeh al-Jami' no. 1751, Irwa' no. 1057)
- Finding a lost child is a communal obligation to take care of. The child is
  considered Muslim and free unless proven otherwise. If found with wealth, it
  may be used for his needs; otherwise maintained from the public treasury.
  (Malik, al-Bayhaqi — Saheeh per al-Albani, Irwa' no. 1573)

[EVIDENCE]:
- Hadith of Ubayy ibn K'ab on money purse (Bukhari, Muslim).
- Hadith of 'Iyad ibn Himar on witnessing (Saheeh Sunan Ibn Majah no. 2032).
- Hadith of Zayd ibn Khalid on sheep/camels (Bukhari, Muslim).
- Hadith on Makkah's inviolability (Saheeh al-Jami' no. 1751).
- Narration of 'Umar on lost child (Irwa' no. 1573).

[STANDARD]: Gift-Giving (al-Hibah)
[CORE TOPIC]: Encouragement to give gifts, accepting gifts, reciprocating,
fairness among children, and the prohibition of taking back gifts.

[PERMISSIBLE CONDITIONS]:
- Gift-giving is encouraged. "O Muslim women, do not belittle the gift sent by
  a neighbour, even if it be a hoof of a lamb." (Bukhari, Muslim)
- "Give gifts to one another and you will love one another." (al-Bayhaqi —
  Saheeh per al-Albani, Saheeh al-Jami' no. 3004, Irwa' no. 1601)
- Accepting even the smallest gifts is encouraged. "If I were invited to a meal
  consisting of the hoof of a lamb, I would accept. And if I were given the hoof
  of a lamb, I would accept." (Bukhari — Saheeh per al-Albani)
- Certain gifts should never be refused: a pillow, milk, and perfume. (Tirmidhi —
  Hasan per al-Albani, Saheeh Sunan Tirmidhi no. 2241)
- The Prophet used to accept gifts and give something in return. (Bukhari, Abu
  Dawood, Tirmidhi)
- Give preference to the nearest neighbour: "Give to the one whose door is
  closest to you." (Bukhari, Abu Dawood)
- Give preference to relatives: "If you have given her to your maternal aunts,
  your reward would have been greater." (Bukhari, Muslim, Abu Dawood)
- Al-'Umra and ar-Ruqba (conditional lifetime gifts): The Prophet made both
  valid regular gifts belonging to the receiver and passed on to his heirs.
  "Whoever makes an 'umra gift, then it is for the one who has been given it,
  alive and dead, and for his descendants." (Muslim — Saheeh per al-Albani,
  Saheeh al-Jami' no. 1388)

[PROHIBITED / INVALID STRUCTURES]:
- It is forbidden to give preference to some children over others in gifts.
  The Prophet said to a father who gifted only one child: "Fear Allah and be
  equitable with respect to your children." He refused to witness it: "I do
  not witness to injustice." (Bukhari, Muslim, Abu Dawood)
- It is not permissible to take back a gift or purchase it back. "The one who
  takes back his gift is like a dog who returns to his vomit." (Bukhari, Muslim,
  Abu Dawood, Tirmidhi, Nasa'i)
- 'Umar wanted to buy back a horse he had given in charity. The Prophet said:
  "Do not buy it even if he is willing to give it to you for one dirham."
  (Bukhari, Muslim)
- Exception: A father may take back what he has given to his child. "It is not
  allowed for a person to give something away and then to take it back, except
  what a father has given to his child." (Abu Dawood, Tirmidhi, Nasa'i, Ibn
  Majah — Saheeh per al-Albani, Saheeh al-Jami' no. 7655)
- Government workers accepting gifts is a kind of stealing. The Prophet
  condemned it: "Why didn't he sit in the house of his mother and father and
  see if he will be given gifts or not?" (Bukhari, Muslim, Abu Dawood)

[EVIDENCE]:
- Hadith of Abu Hurayrah on gifts (Bukhari, Muslim).
- Hadith on giving gifts to love each other (Saheeh al-Jami' no. 3004).
- Hadith of an-Nu'man ibn Basheer on fairness (Bukhari, Muslim).
- Hadith of Ibn 'Abbas on taking back gifts (Bukhari, Muslim).
- Hadith on government workers and gifts (Bukhari, Muslim).

[STANDARD]: Usurping Others' Property and Rights (al-Ghasb)
[CORE TOPIC]: The prohibition of wrongfully taking others' property, land
usurpation, self-defence of property, and preemption rights.

[PROHIBITED / INVALID STRUCTURES]:
- Usurping others' property is a form of wrongdoing (dhulm) and wrongdoing
  will be darkness on the Day of Resurrection. (Qur'an 14:42-43, 2:188)
- The Prophet said in his Farewell Speech: "Verily, your blood, wealth and
  honour are inviolable to all of you." (Muslim — Saheeh per al-Albani, Saheeh
  al-Jami' no. 2068)
- "No one should take the possession of his brother, jokingly or seriously. If
  one has taken the staff of his brother, he should return it to him." (Abu
  Dawood, Tirmidhi — Hasan per al-Albani, Saheeh al-Jami' no. 7578)
- Whoever has wronged another must beg forgiveness before the Day of Resurrection
  when good deeds will be taken to compensate. (Bukhari, Tirmidhi — Saheeh per
  al-Albani, Saheeh al-Jami' no. 6511)
- It is forbidden to use or benefit from what one has usurped. It is obligatory
  to return it.
- Usurping land: "Whoever wrongfully takes a piece of land, on the Day of
  Judgment his neck will be encircled with it throughout the seven earths."
  (Bukhari, Muslim) "Whoever takes a piece of land without due right will be
  sunk down through the seven earths on the Day of Resurrection." (Bukhari —
  Saheeh per al-Albani, Saheeh al-Jami' no. 6385)
- If someone usurps land and grows crops or builds on it, he must root out what
  he planted or tear down what he built. "What the wrongdoer has planted, he
  has no rights for it." (Tirmidhi, al-Bayhaqi — Saheeh per al-Albani)
- "Whoever farms in a land of a people without their permission shall not be
  due what he farmed but for him is what he expended." (Tirmidhi, Ibn Majah)

[PERMISSIBLE CONDITIONS]:
- A person may defend his life and wealth. "Do not give him your wealth... fight
  him... if he kills you, you are a martyr... if you kill him, he will be in the
  Fire." (Muslim, Nasa'i — Saheeh per al-Albani, Mukhtasar no. 1086)

[EVIDENCE]:
- Qur'an: 14:42-43, 2:188.
- Hadith of Farewell Speech on inviolability (Saheeh al-Jami' no. 2068).
- Hadith on land usurpation (Bukhari, Muslim, Saheeh al-Jami' no. 6385).
- Hadith of Abu Hurayrah on self-defence (Muslim, Mukhtasar no. 1086).

[STANDARD]: Preemption (ash-Shufa')
[CORE TOPIC]: The partner's right of first refusal when property is sold, and
the neighbour's preemption right when they share common rights.

[PERMISSIBLE CONDITIONS]:
- The Prophet judged by the rule of preemption on every property that had not
  been subdivided. If the boundaries are demarcated and the roadways fixed,
  then there is no preemption. (Bukhari, Abu Dawood, Ibn Majah — Saheeh per
  al-Albani, Saheeh Sunan Ibn Majah no. 2028)
- If a person is a partner in land, a wall, or a house, the property cannot be
  sold until it is first offered to the partner. If sold without offering, the
  partner still has the right to buy it. "If a person owns date-palms or a piece
  of land, he is not to sell it until he offers it to his partner." (Ibn Majah,
  Nasa'i — Saheeh per al-Albani, Saheeh Sunan Ibn Majah no. 2021)
- "The partner has more right due to his closeness regardless of what is
  involved." (Ibn Majah — Saheeh per al-Albani, Saheeh Sunan Ibn Majah no. 2027)
- If two neighbours share some rights (roadway, water), each has the right of
  preemption. "The neighbour has the most right for preemption with his
  neighbour. One should wait for him if he is absent. This applies if their
  roadway is one." (Abu Dawood, Tirmidhi, Ibn Majah — Saheeh per al-Albani,
  Saheeh Sunan Ibn Majah no. 2023)

[EVIDENCE]:
- Hadith of Jabir on preemption (Bukhari, Saheeh Sunan Ibn Majah no. 2028).
- Hadith on offering to partner first (Saheeh Sunan Ibn Majah no. 2021).
- Hadith on neighbour's preemption (Saheeh Sunan Ibn Majah no. 2023).

[STANDARD]: Agency and Power of Attorney (al-Wakalah)
[CORE TOPIC]: Appointing another to represent oneself in transactions and
other affairs, its permissibility, and the agent's trustee status.

[PERMISSIBLE CONDITIONS]:
- Wakalah (agency) is sanctioned by the Qur'an (18:19 — story of the People
  of the Cave sending one to buy food), Sunnah, and consensus.
- Power of attorney is permissible in any kind of dealing or disposition that
  an individual is allowed to conclude on his own behalf. It is permissible
  to delegate authority to others or to accept delegation from others.
- It is a recommended act as it is a form of aiding one another in piety and
  righteousness, since not everyone can directly fulfill all of his affairs.
- Abu Rafi' said the Prophet sent him as a messenger (agent) between himself
  and Maymoonah regarding their marriage. (Ahmad, ad-Darimi — Saheeh per
  al-Albani, Irwa' al-Ghaleel vol. 6 p. 252)
- The Prophet gave others authority to act on his behalf in repaying debts,
  implementing prescribed punishments, and other acts.

[PROHIBITED / INVALID STRUCTURES]:
- Agency for haram transactions is not permitted.
- The agent is a trustee and is NOT liable for anything unless he himself was
  negligent. "There is no liability upon the one who accepts a trust." (Saheeh
  al-Jami' no. 7518)

[EVIDENCE]:
- Qur'an: 18:19.
- Hadith of Abu Rafi' on agency in marriage (Irwa' vol. 6 p. 252).
- Hadith on trustee liability (Saheeh al-Jami' no. 7518).
- Consensus of Muslims on permissibility.
